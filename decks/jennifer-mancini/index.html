EMAIL 02

Subject: You don’t need to become a gym rat

Sarah,

There’s a version of “getting your body back” that I wouldn’t recommend to you.

Cutting out your favorite foods. Living on salads and cardio. Signing up for a 30-day challenge that only works for as long as your willpower does. Turning your mornings into another full-time job.

That’s the version most women picture when they hear “weight loss after 40.” And it’s exactly why a lot of busy women write it off, or try it, get frustrated, and decide it’s just them.

But that’s not the model we’re talking about.

The approach is a simple daily metabolic reset inside a structured plan built around your body. Training, nutrition, and recovery work together instead of competing for your time and energy. You don’t need to quit the foods you enjoy, and you don’t need to become a gym rat to start.

That’s also why the “simple daily” part matters. You don’t need to overhaul your life to test it. You need a routine you can follow consistently.

And if your objection is “I’ve already tried everything,” that’s a fair thing to feel. The better question is whether you’ve tried something built for you, or a generic plan built for everyone. Most plans treat every woman the same. Your history, your goals, and the way your body responds are not the same.

So before our call, watch the VSL with that distinction in mind. Don’t ask whether this is “easy.” Ask whether this particular approach fits your schedule, your lifestyle, and your goals.

Subject: Linda, 47, school administrator, sleeping through the night by week 3

Sarah,

I want to tell you about Linda.

If there’s one story I’d want you to read before our call, it’s hers.

Linda is 47, from Charlotte, North Carolina, married with two kids. She’s a school administrator, works about 45 hours a week, and was looking for a way to feel like herself again without adding another thing to her schedule.

Before she found us, she’d tried a couple of 30-day challenges and a calorie-tracking app. Nothing stuck. She was exhausted by 2 p.m., waking up in the middle of the night, and never knew what to do next.

She joined in January and started with about 10 minutes a day, before the kids woke up. By week two she was sleeping more soundly. By week six her afternoon crashes were gone and her clothes fit differently. By month three she was still eating the foods she loved and had a routine she no longer had to think about.

Linda didn’t have a gym membership, a free hour, or any interest in giving up pasta. She had a full-time job and 10 minutes.

A few weeks in, she told me something I think about a lot: “Before, I was guessing every day. Now I know what to do, and I actually do it.”

I picked Linda instead of our biggest results because she’s the closest thing we have to the typical woman on these calls. Busy. Skeptical. Tired of trying things alone.

If you see yourself in that, her path is the realistic version of what’s possible. Not an overnight story. Steady work, the right system, and a coach who tells you the next move.

We’ll map out what your version looks like on our call.

— Jennifer
