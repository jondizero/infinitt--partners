RECOMMENDATION 01

Separate your customer journeys based on who you're trying to serve.

Instead of making one homepage explain everything to everyone, create distinct entry points for each audience. Each journey should speak directly to that buyer's situation, present the relevant offer, and guide them toward the appropriate next step. The goal is to make every visitor feel like they've landed in the right place, with an offer designed for their specific situation.

  • Give each audience its own landing page with a specific promise and relevant messaging.
  • Present only the services and outcomes that matter to that particular buyer.
  • Build separate calls to action and follow-up sequences based on the audience's interest.

RECOMMENDATION 02

Start selling valuable outcomes instead of cheap monthly retainers

Your offers should reflect the problems you're helping customers solve and the value of getting those problems addressed. This creates room for higher-ticket services without abandoning the lower-priced products that help people discover your brand.

  • Package your expertise into focused programs with defined outcomes and deliverables.
  • Create premium consulting or implementation offers for clients who need more direct involvement.
  • Use the Skool membership as an entry-level community that introduces people to your higher-value services.

OBSERVATION 03

Your qualification form is creating too much friction

Your qualification form asks for a lot of information upfront. Adding friction can help filter out people who aren't serious, but there's a point where the form becomes so demanding that even genuinely interested prospects may abandon it.

  • The form creates a demanding first impression.
  • Prospects may lose interest before completing the application.
  • You're missing an opportunity to make the qualification process feel personalized and engaging.

RECOMMENDATION 03

Turn the qualification form into a shorter, or at least, interactive experience.

Instead of presenting prospects with a long list of questions all at once, guide them through a series of smaller steps. This preserves the qualification process while making it feel easier and more engaging to complete.

  • Break the form into short stages
  • Use conditional logic to show additional questions only when they're relevant.
  • Route qualified prospects toward the next step while directing others to more appropriate resources.
