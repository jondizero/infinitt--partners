If I were learning multifamily real estate from scratch, I wouldn't start by asking how to buy the biggest deal. I'd start by studying how experienced investors actually got their first one. The useful lessons usually aren't just the headline numbers. They're the decisions behind them: — How they found the opportunity — How they evaluated the deal — Who they learned from — What they got wrong early — How they built a business that could scale That's why conversations with operators are so valuable. You get to hear the path, not just the polished result. A good question to ask any experienced investor: “What changed between your first deal and the business you run today?” The answer can teach you more than another hour of generic advice. Learn from people who've done it. Then build your own process.

A useful way to study multifamily investing is to look at the decisions behind the outcome.

Look at how experienced operators source deals, evaluate risk, build relationships, and make decisions when the numbers do not work.

A conversation with an experienced investor can make those steps easier to understand than a list of disconnected tips.

The aim is not to copy another investor's path, but to understand the principles behind it.

The useful lessons are in the decisions behind each deal: sourcing, underwriting, financing, partnerships, and the point at which an operator decides to scale.

You do not need to know every answer before you start learning. You need a reliable way to study real examples and turn them into better questions.

Subject: How to learn from an investor's first deal

Hi there,

One of the most useful things to study is how an investor's first deal shaped the business they built.

When you listen to an experienced operator, look beyond the polished result and pay attention to the sequence of decisions.

How did they find the opportunity? What did they misunderstand at first? Which relationships mattered? What would they avoid if they were starting again?

Those questions help turn an interview from inspiration into a practical case study.

You can use the same approach with each Multifamily Hour conversation: capture the starting point, the turning points, the mistakes, and the principles the investor would carry into their next deal.

You do not need to copy another investor's path exactly. You need to understand the reasoning well enough to make your own decisions.

The best takeaway from any interview is a question you can apply to your own situation.
