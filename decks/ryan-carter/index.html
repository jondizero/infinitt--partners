Subject

"I've already tried everything"

Body

The most common thing I hear before a consultation, {{first_name}}, is some version of this: "I've already tried everything." The diets. The supplements. The elimination plans. The appointments. The apps. The tests. The periods of being extremely disciplined, followed by the point where it became impossible to keep doing all of it. I understand why you would be sceptical. If you have spent months or years trying to improve your health and are still dealing with the same pattern, another person telling you to "just be consistent" is not particularly helpful. But "I've tried everything" often means something more specific: you tried many individual things without a clear process for understanding what was actually happening. You changed several variables at once, so you couldn't tell what helped. You were given a plan without enough explanation to adapt it. You stopped when the first improvement plateaued because nobody helped you decide what to do next. This is not a criticism of you. It's what happens when people are handed isolated recommendations instead of a structured, personalised process. My approach keeps returning to the evidence of your own response: what changed, what didn't, what became easier, what became harder, what needs adjusting. In the Foundational Programme, that means building a practical base around nutrition, sleep, stress, movement, digestion, and daily habits. In the Advanced Programme, it may also involve a more detailed health history, functional testing where appropriate, and a phased plan for complex or long-standing symptoms. Testing isn't automatically the answer, and more supplements aren't automatically the answer either. The answer is a clearer process: assess properly, start with the highest-priority changes, implement them, review the response, adjust, then build from there. I'll be direct with you on the call. If I think your situation is outside my scope, or the programme isn't the right next step, I'll say so. But if you've been trying random strategies without a coherent plan, it may be that you've never had the right structure around the work. Ryan P.S. Please bring a list of the main things you've already tried, so we can spend the call on what's actually relevant.

Subject

The goal is not a perfect protocol

Body

One last thought before we speak, {{first_name}}. People rarely book a consultation because they want another document full of health information. They book because they want their life back — waking up with energy instead of negotiating with exhaustion, eating without wondering what happens afterwards, training and working and travelling without their symptoms dictating the day. That's why the programme is built around more than nutrition. Food matters, but so do sleep, stress, movement, recovery, behaviour, environment, and how all of those interact with your particular history. A perfect protocol you can't live with isn't a health solution. A sustainable plan that helps you understand your body and make better decisions in ordinary life is far more valuable. This is also why I talk about partnership. You know your body and daily reality better than anyone else. I bring the clinical training, the framework, the questions, the analysis, and the process for turning information into practical action. Neither side works properly without the other. My promise isn't that every symptom disappears on a particular date — that wouldn't be honest, and health doesn't work that way. My promise is that, if we decide to work together, I'll take the time to understand your situation, give you a clear plan, support you through implementation, and adjust the strategy when the evidence tells us to. The destination isn't becoming obsessed with health. It's having enough health, energy, confidence, and understanding that you can get on with your life. We'll talk soon, Ryan P.S.
