Every so often, someone declares Amazon dead, wholesale too saturated, or says there's no room left for new sellers.

That headline skips the more useful question: what kind of business model are you actually looking at, and how are you making decisions?

Amazon wholesale isn't the same as inventing a product and hoping people want it. The model centers on established brands, legitimate supplier relationships, account requirements, and evaluating products where demand already exists.

That doesn't make it easy or automatic. Suppliers choose who they approve, products have restrictions, and the numbers only work when you account for the real costs — Amazon fees, shipping, prep, storage, and purchase price.

But “the market is saturated” isn't a substitute for learning how to assess those factors. The useful work is building a repeatable process: research carefully, understand supplier requirements, and check the economics before committing capital.

Other paths can require a different mix of time, capital, audience-building, or risk. The right choice depends on your situation — not on a blanket claim that one model is dead.
