OBSERVATION 01 · VSL

Proof is doing the heavy lifting. The mechanism isn't.

Social proof can attract attention, but skeptical prospects still need a logical explanation for how the result is produced. Without that framework, impressive results can feel like exceptions rather than a process someone can learn and repeat.

  • Over four minutes of a seven-minute VSL are devoted to proof, based on the supplied analysis.
  • The method itself is not explained clearly enough.
  • Objections remain unanswered even after the results are shown.

RECOMMENDATION 01 · VSL

Give the proof a framework that makes it believable.

Restructure the VSL around an “Old Way vs. New Way” narrative. Explain the wholesale mechanism first, dismantle the most common objections, and then use proof to demonstrate the process in action.

  • Build belief before stacking more testimonials.
  • Explain how sourcing, product selection, and unit economics fit together.
  • Connect each proof point to a specific step in the method.

OBSERVATION 02 · LEAD CAPTURE

The funnel asks for a purchase before it captures the prospect.

Sending everyone directly to checkout means visitors who need more time can disappear without leaving a way to follow up. The funnel currently has no low-friction step to preserve that interest.

  • No lead capture before checkout.
  • Non-buyers cannot be nurtured through email.
  • Retargeting and follow-up opportunities are limited.

RECOMMENDATION 02 · LEAD CAPTURE

Keep the conversation going when they're not ready to buy.

Add a simple email capture or short application step before checkout, then create a follow-up path for people who opt in but don't purchase. Keep the extra step lightweight so it doesn't create unnecessary friction.

  • Build a retargetable, permission-based audience.
  • Recover interested non-buyers with useful follow-up.
  • Learn where visitors drop out instead of losing the signal.

OBSERVATION 03 · PRICING

The price doesn't reflect the amount of access being offered.

At $2,500 for a full year of 1:1 mentorship, the model risks undervaluing Marvin's time and making high-touch support difficult to deliver profitably. The 3-month and 12-month options also don't create much separation in perceived value.

  • High-touch delivery can become unsustainable at this price.
  • Price is framed around duration, not the value of the transformation.
  • Too much access may be promised without clear boundaries.

RECOMMENDATION 03 · OFFER & PRICING

Price the transformation — and define the access.

Separate the core system from premium 1:1 access. Make the deliverables, milestones, boundaries, and support cadence explicit, then price each tier around the value and capacity it requires rather than simply adding months.

  • Protect delivery quality and margin.
  • Make premium support feel genuinely premium.
  • Serve more clients without making every client a 1:1 obligation.

OBSERVATION 04 · MOBILE

The first screen feels unfinished on mobile.

The page leaves a large empty area above the content while the video is cut off at the bottom of the first screen. Neither issue has to be a major conversion blocker alone, but together they make the opening experience feel less intentional.

  • Limited screen space is not being used efficiently.
  • The main video is less visible at the moment it matters.
  • The page loses visual momentum before the pitch begins.

RECOMMENDATION 04 · MOBILE

Make the first screen feel deliberate.

Reduce top spacing, bring the headline and video into a tighter hierarchy, and test the opening viewport on real mobile dimensions. Keep the primary action visible without forcing the visitor to scroll past empty space.

  • Remove excess top padding.
  • Resize or reposition the video to show more above the fold.
  • Check small phones and larger mobile screens.
