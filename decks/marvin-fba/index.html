EMAIL 01 · MECHANISM
Subject: Amazon wholesale isn't about inventing a product

Hey {{first_name}},

One thing worth understanding about Amazon wholesale: you don't necessarily need to create a product of your own.

The model is built around working with established brands and legitimate wholesale suppliers, getting approved to buy their products, and then evaluating whether the numbers make sense for resale.

That doesn't mean every product is a winner or every supplier will approve you. It means there's a process to learn — and understanding that process comes before buying inventory.

Tomorrow, I'll share one practical part of that process: how to approach supplier outreach without treating it like a magic script.

— Marvin

EMAIL 02 · PRACTICAL VALUE
Subject: Your supplier email has one job

Hey {{first_name}},

Your first email to a distributor doesn't need to tell your life story. It needs to make it easy for them to understand who you are, what you're asking for, and why they should take the next step.

Keep it direct: introduce your business, ask about the process for opening a wholesale account, and offer whatever business details or documentation they require. Follow the supplier's actual requirements rather than trying to force a response with a magic script.

And don't treat silence as a verdict on the whole model. Some suppliers may not be a fit; others have specific criteria or a process you need to follow.

The goal is to build a repeatable outreach process — not send the same message blindly to everyone.

— Marvin

EMAIL 03 · NEXT STEP
Subject: Before you buy inventory, get clear on these three things

Hey {{first_name}},

Before placing an order, make sure you've answered three questions:

1. Is the supplier legitimate and appropriate for your business?
Confirm their requirements and the terms of the account.

2. Do the product economics make sense?
Account for Amazon fees, shipping, prep, storage, and the actual purchase cost — not just the difference between two prices.

3. Do you understand the restrictions?
Check the brand, category, and product requirements before committing capital.

Wholesale is a process of decisions. Learning to evaluate each one matters more than rushing to make the first sale.

Most people think starting an Amazon business means inventing a product. It doesn't have to. Amazon wholesale is a different model: → Work with established brands → Apply to legitimate wholesale suppliers → Find products with existing demand → Check the numbers before buying inventory → Resell through the right channels The hard part isn't finding a “winning product” on a random list. It's building a repeatable process for supplier approval, product research, and buying decisions. No business model is automatic, and supplier approval isn't guaranteed. But if you're researching Amazon, understand the model before you decide which path to take. Start with the process. Then decide if it's right for you.
