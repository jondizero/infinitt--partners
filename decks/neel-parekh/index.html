OBSERVATION 01

You're qualifying interest, not buying potential.

Your form collects basic information, but it doesn't establish the prospect's financial position or level of experience. That means you're accepting applications without knowing whether someone has the resources, background, or business foundation to benefit from your offer.

  • You spend time reviewing leads without knowing their financial fit.
  • Prospects with very different levels of experience enter the same pipeline.
  • You have less context to prioritize the applicants most likely to become customers.

RECOMMENDATION 01

Qualify prospects before investing time in them

Build an application that reveals whether each prospect has the financial capacity, experience, and circumstances to be a strong fit.

  • Ask about relevant experience and current business situation.
  • Include financial qualification questions appropriate to the offer.
  • Use the responses to prioritize stronger opportunities and personalize follow-up.
0620
OBSERVATION 02

You're collecting leads, then leaving them at a dead end.

After prospects complete your form, they see only a thank-you message, with no clear next step. You've already earned their attention and collected their information, but the funnel stops instead of guiding them toward a conversation, an offer, or another meaningful action.

  • High-intent prospects receive no immediate direction on what to do next.
  • You lose an opportunity to turn initial interest into further engagement.
  • Leads can go cold while waiting for your next communication.

OBSERVATION 03

You're leaving an entire market of buyers unserved.

Your current offer ladder jumps from a $799 course straight to a $60K franchise, leaving a massive range of potential buyers without an offer that fits their budget. There are people who would happily invest $3K, $5K, $7K, or even $15K to get more support and better results, but right now, there's nothing in your portfolio designed to capture that demand.

  • You're missing an entire segment of buyers who can afford more than your course but aren't ready to invest $60K.
  • Prospects who could become 3K–15K customers have no natural next step with you.
  • Even a modest number of sales in this price range could add 50K–100K+ in monthly revenue potential.

RECOMMENDATION 03

Capture more value from the middle of your market

Expand your offer ladder to serve buyers who want a deeper level of support but aren't ready for a franchise investment.

  • Create an offer that solves a meaningful problem beyond what the $799 course delivers.
  • Test price points such as $3K, $5K, $7K, and $15K against actual buyer demand.
  • Establish an upgrade path that allows successful customers to progress toward the franchise over time.

OBSERVATION 04

You're adding friction at the moment of purchase.

Your course checkout only offers credit card payments, excluding alternatives such as Apple Pay and Google Pay. Even when someone is ready to buy, a payment process that doesn't support their preferred method introduces another obstacle between intent and completed purchase.

  • Some buyers may abandon checkout rather than enter their card details manually.
  • Mobile buyers lose the convenience of supported digital wallets.
  • You risk losing sales over payment friction rather than a lack of interest in the course.

RECOMMENDATION 04

Make it easier to complete the purchase

Offer the payment methods your customers are most likely to use, while keeping the checkout simple and secure.

  • Enable Apple Pay and Google Pay where supported by your payment provider.
  • Review payment-method availability across devices and browsers.
  • Track checkout completion and payment failures to identify where friction remains.

Your course checkout offers the main product without an order bump or relevant complementary purchase. That means buyers who might be willing to spend more to get additional resources or support have no immediate way to add them to their order.

  • Every customer is limited to the core purchase unless they buy something later.
  • You miss a chance to increase average order value from existing purchase intent.
  • Generating more revenue depends more heavily on acquiring additional customers.
